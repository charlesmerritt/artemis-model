The object being corrected 02

One whole FIA plot is stamped onto every forested pixel — where LANDFIRE allows it

TreeMap is a 30 m raster of imputed FIA plot identifiers (TM_ID → PLT_CN). It is a map of which plot best matches here — not a map of measured values. Basal area, volume, carbon and the tree list are all carried over from that plot.

How it is built Riley / Houtman lineage

Modified Random Forest imputation via R yaImpute, ~249 trees per LANDFIRE zone (83 per response variable). Each pixel receives the single plot that co-occurs with it most often in the terminal nodes — k = 1, one whole plot per pixel.

Predictors: LANDFIRE EVC/EVH/EVT, disturbance and time-since, terrain, lat/lon, Daymet normals. Reference: single-condition forested FIA plots, 1997–2022, true unfuzzed coordinates.

The dependency that drives everything the load-bearing fact

TreeMap assigns a TM_ID only where LANDFIRE EVT already says "forest." Everywhere else it writes nothing at all — a hole.

So TreeMap does not merely inherit LANDFIRE's classification noise; it inherits LANDFIRE's entire definition of where forest exists. If EVT drops a stand, TreeMap has no representation of it whatsoever — not a wrong value, but absence.

Forty percent of the AOI has no TreeMap value at all

Most of the hole area is genuinely not forest — pasture, row crop, developed, marsh, open water. The question is not whether holes are legitimate in general, but whether a specific, identifiable subset of them is managed forest caught mid-rotation.

Definitional care: the ArcGIS-exported holes raster is not clipped to the AOI — it reads 1 across the whole bounding box (10.66M px, only 3.28M of them real in-AOI holes). The usable hole universe is the shared valid-data footprint of the change rasters, 3,282,389 px. Using the raw export would inflate every downstream fraction by 3×.

Correction 1 — root cause 05

LANDFIRE stopped populating its harvest flag, and TreeMap had no fallback

The three Recently Logged classes (7191 Herb, 7192 Shrub, 7193 Tree) exist in the legend of every vintage. They are populated in 2016 and empty in 2022 and 2024. Those pixels are now Southeastern Ruderal Grassland and Pasture/Hayland — herb lifeforms, so TreeMap imputes nothing there.

Correction 1 — the real scope 06

This is a LANDFIRE lag problem, and it costs us an entire age class

The 6–9 year blind spot bigger than the 2022 vintage

78% of the high-confidence acreage is S1 — 41,549 of the 52,979 ac where LANDFIRE brackets a hole with forest at both endpoints. S1 is the group LANDFIRE had already flagged Recently Logged back in 2016: cut around or before 2016, still mapped as ruderal grassland in 2022, and only re-entering the forest classes in LF2024.

So EVT takes roughly 6–9 years to re-recognise a replanted pine stand. TreeMap is not just missing stands cut near 2022 — it is systematically missing the entire 0–8 year age class of managed pine.

For an even-flow harvest scheduler, that is precisely the cohort determining the next rotation's supply. Its absence biases the whole schedule.

Why you cannot just copy a neighbour Finding 6

Plots imputed in the AOI by FIA stand-size class: 359 large-diameter, 261 medium, 255 small — and 1 nonstocked. There is effectively no young-stand donor in TreeMap's local imputation universe, by construction: TreeMap can only impute where LANDFIRE already says forest.

Older vintages do not rescue it either. TreeMap 2020 covers only 3.1% of the strong strata — it was built on LANDFIRE 2020, which had already relabelled these stands. The holes are inherited across vintages.

The fix must synthesise a young stand, not copy one.

Needs verification: the exact window LANDFIRE means by "Recently Logged" is assumed to be roughly 0–10 years post-harvest, not confirmed against LANDFIRE documentation.

Correction 1 — stratification 07

The holes stratify cleanly on multi-vintage LANDFIRE evidence

Forest evidence in 2016 = Tree lifeform (excluding urban/orchard tree classes TreeMap excludes by design) or any Recently Logged class. Cross that against LF2024.

S1+S2 is the unambiguous signature — hole in 2022, forest at both ends. LF2022 calls it Southeastern Ruderal Grassland (66%); LF2024 calls it Forest Plantation, Interior Upland Longleaf Pine Woodland and Atlantic Coastal Plain Upland Longleaf. That is silviculture, not land-use change.

Correction 1 — method 08

A two-stage funnel, with the leakage traps closed on purpose

Stage A narrows the universe by embedding similarity; Stage B decides. A hole is proposed for add-back only if it clears both.

Stage A — similarity mask recall-oriented

Cluster the AlphaEarth embeddings of S1+S2 anchors into 6 exemplars; score every hole by cosine similarity. The threshold is not hand-picked — --recall sets the anchor fraction retained and the threshold is that quantile (0.9046 at 90%).

Multi-exemplar made Stage A more selective, not less: at equal 90% anchor recall, stable non-forest leakage fell 30.3% → 11.6%.

Stage B — binary classifier deliberately hard negatives

Logistic regression, S1+S2 anchors against S5 restricted to herb / agriculture / shrub. Water, developed and barren are excluded from the negatives on purpose — they make the problem trivially easy and teach nothing about the pasture-vs-young-plantation boundary that actually matters.

The fitted model is folded into a single dot product plus intercept and pushed back to Earth Engine, so the exported raster is the fitted model rather than a re-fit. A test pins that algebra to 1e-9.

  • Feature years capped at 2022, enforced with a raise. The anchor label is defined by LF2024 calling the pixel forest, so a 2023/24 embedding lets the model read the label off the feature — the same trap that drove AUC to 1.000 "largely by construction" in the earlier statewide study.
  • GroupKFold on 0.25° blocks + a label-shuffle baseline printed beside every score; the run warns if real and shuffled AUC are within 0.15.
  • S1/S2 are never re-scored by the model. They are the training positives and their forest status is already proven by LANDFIRE regrowth. Scoring your own anchors is circular; letting the model overrule ground evidence is worse.

Correction 1 — the fix that mattered 09

The generalisation gap was predicted, then measured, then closed

S1 anchors were cut ~2014–2016, so in a 2022 embedding they are 6–8 year old pine. A fresh clearcut looks nothing like that — and S3 is mostly fresh. The first run made the gap visible instead of hiding it in an average.

Correction 1 — independent check 11

Against design-based FIA, the correction moves TreeMap into parity

  • Published TreeMap significantly under-maps forest — 1,094,686 ac falls below the FIA 95% lower bound of 1,106,653. The 160,738 ac shortfall is 2.12 SE.
  • The corrected value lands inside the interval (1,170,517 ac) and below the point estimate, so detection was not too permissive. It could have overshot 1,255,424 ac; it did not.
  • The residual is not a finding. 84,907 ac is 1.12 SE — indistinguishable from zero. This reconciliation cannot establish that more forest remains to be recovered, and an earlier draft that quoted "47% of the shortfall closed" as a result was corrected.

Correction 1 — why the domain query matters 12

Asking EVALIDator the right question changes what the check can claim

The FIA reconciliation is the only genuinely independent check on total corrected forest area, so the number behind it does not rest on one hand-written SQL query against a local FIADB copy. verify_fia_evalidator.py re-derives it from the USDA FIADB-API and compares.

Two derivations, one answer 0.04 ac apart

Local SQL: Σ (CONDPROP_UNADJ × ADJ_FACTOR × EXPNS) over COND_STATUS_CD = 1, with ADJ_FACTOR selected by PROP_BASIS.

EVALIDator: attribute 2 "Area of forest land, in acres", evaluation group 122022, wf=PLOT.COUNTYCD IN (3,23,47,121,125).

Both return 1,255,424 ac. The API also returns the sampling error the local SQL cannot: SE 6.046% (± 75,903 ac), 262 plots.

Domain, not summed counties a real statistical trap

County estimates within one evaluation share strata. Combining their variances in quadrature assumes an independence that does not hold — that approximation gives ± 80,718 ac against the correct ± 75,903 ac.

The wider, wrong interval would have made the corrected value look comfortably central and the residual look smaller relative to error. The correct interval is what forces the honest statement that the residual is 1.12 SE and cannot be interpreted.

Framing caveat, carried from the methodology note: a pixel-count area is not the FIA population estimator, so equality was never expected. The test is one of direction and interval membership, not of agreement. County-level FIA has only 262 contributing plots, which is why the interval is ± 6% wide.

Correction 1 — second independent check 13

A different producer, a different algorithm, and it agrees

USFS LCMS encodes exactly what TreeMap loses: a stand cut in 2021 is still Forest land use in 2022 even when its cover is grass. 600 points per group, drawn only from one-pixel-eroded interiors of components ≥ 5 ac, with S1 and S5 as reference bookends.

  • Accepted interiors are strongly corroborated — 0.98 LCMS forest land use, against a 0.078 S5 floor.
  • LCMS independently confirms the LANDFIRE lag. 88% of accepted S3 is LCMS Trees by 2024 despite S3 being defined as not-tree in LANDFIRE 2024. Two products disagree in the predicted direction, and nothing engineered that.
  • LCMS Tree Removal is unusable here — higher on rejected S3 (0.408) than accepted (0.302), confirming the prior from the earlier statewide study.
Do not over-read these. The frame covers only 15.9% of accepted and 9.9% of rejected S3 acreage, so 0.98 and the 0.329 recall proxy are sampling-frame proxies, not population measurements. No design-valid interval was computed for a spatially correlated pixel sample, and LCMS shares optical sensors with AlphaEarth — independent in producer and algorithm, not in imagery. Representative hand-labelled NAIP remains the highest-value next step.

Correction 1 — lineage 14

The statewide embedding study called the target, then got superseded

The earlier clearcut-vs-agriculture work sampled 1,700 points across Florida to ask which EVT classes mislabel cut forest. It was right about the target and wrong about the constraint.

What it got right confirmed independently

It ranked Southeastern Ruderal Grassland (9823) as the class most often mislabelling clearcut forest — and 66% of S1+S2 is exactly that class in LF2022. Independent agreement from a different method and sample.

It also flagged Pasture/Hayland (7997) as genuinely agricultural, which the strata support.

The constraint that turned out false superseded

That study concluded there was no clean multi-vintage EVT diff: Earth Engine carries only the ~2016 pre-Remap vintage, so it bridged at the coarse "forest vs not" level using class-name keywords.

The local LF2016_EVT_CONUS download is LF 2.0.0 "2016 Remap" — its codes match LF2022 and LF2024 exactly (7997, 9823, 9322; 1,046 shared class names). Do the EVT change analysis locally, not on GEE.

The trap it inherited turned into a guard

Its AUC ≈ 1.000 was "largely by construction" — positives defined as pre-year forest, and the pre-year embedding encoding exactly that.

That diagnosis became the explicit MAX_FEATURE_YEAR = 2022 guard in the hole pipeline, which raises rather than warns. A known-bad outcome became a hard constraint.

The other error mode 15

Where the forest is mapped, two error channels remain

Hole rectification fixes missing forest. Inside the mapped forest, pixel-count expansion (acres ≈ Count × 0.2224) still diverges from FIA in two separable ways — and the standard fix only touches the first.

(a) Area misallocation correctable

Imputation may paint a plot onto more or fewer pixels than its design weight (EXPNS) warrants.

Fix: area-scale by FIA forest-type acres. Detect over-concentration with the pixel-weighted plot distribution and effective sample size.

(b) Within-type attribute bias not correctable that way

Even at exactly the right acreage, the plots chosen inside a forest type can be systematically denser or sparser than the FIA population for that type.

Area-scaling does not touch this. It has to be measured per FORTYPCD and reported.

The code exists and has never been run in-repo. r/07_FL_FIA_TreeMap_comparison.R implements the decomposition — rFIA design-based vs TreeMap pixel-weighted per FORTYPCD, with ESS, z-scores, and a before/after area-scaling bias decomposition (mean-balancing after Iles 2009). It is R outside the Python pipeline and writes to an uncommitted output/. The hole work validated total area; nothing has yet validated per-type attributes.

Correction 3 17

Three integrity bugs that quietly deleted or scrambled pixels

None of these announce themselves. Each produces a plausible-looking raster that is wrong, which is why they belong in the correction stage rather than in debugging folklore.

Category strings for IDs fixed

terra::rast() auto-loads the TreeMap VAT, so freq() can return active category strings such as ForTypName instead of integer TM_ID values.

Rule: always count on as.int(); never trust VAT display names.

FIA control numbers overflow fixed

PLT_CN exceeds R double precision, so numeric handling corrupts the join key itself — the identifier survives, but points at the wrong plot.

Rule: keep PLT_CN, CN, Stand_CN, StandID as strings end to end.

Label-0 collision fixed + test

skimage's felzenszwalb() labels from 0, unlike slic(). Non-forest was masked to 0 and vectorisation skipped 0 as nodata — so whichever forest segment happened to be labelled 0 was silently dropped before any measurement.

Recovered: 52 pixels · 4.68 ha · 14 patches. Post-erase forest now matches SLIC exactly (27,806.344 ha).

Corrections 5 & 6 19

Two corrections that are decided but not built

The erase layer deletes forest we must grow unbuilt

sketch_management_units.py unions stream/BMP buffers, waterbodies and the road buffer into one erase layer and differences it out. Buffer acres are discarded — neither managed nor grown. That is an under-count of standing volume and carbon, not a conservative choice.

Decided regime: grow freely, never harvested, reported as unique buffer polygons with their own IDs and rows.

Σ managed + Σ riparian ==
(forest ∩ parcels) − (waterbodies ∪ road buffer)

The raw forested AOI is the wrong right-hand side — roads run through forest. Report the permanently excluded area as its own line so the drop stays visible.

Plot-keyed regimes smear the harvest design settled

If the regime is keyed to the plot, managing a plot manages every pixel imputed to that plot. TM_ID 2623 alone covers 1,385 pixels — a clearcut would appear as speckle across the AOI instead of a contiguous block.

The fix is to split the keys: regime assigned per pixel from the management unit; trajectories keyed by (plot_id, regime, site-index bin).

The unit decides what treatment; the plot decides what tree list.

Cost to watch: run count is unique(plot × regime × SI bin), so every regime parameterisation multiplies it. Keep the regime library small and discrete.

Per-acre semantics carry through rule

TreeMap stores per-acre densities, never per-pixel totals — and so do the FVS values we paint. Fine for maps, quantiles and pixel-wise change; totals require × pixel acres (900 m² = 0.2224 ac). Aggregations are area-weighted means, never naive pixel means.

Resolution and codec, both pinned gotcha

The hole model works at AlphaEarth's native 10 m; the product is 30 m to match TreeMap. Agreement is r = 0.988 (probability) / 0.991 (similarity) with 97.7% decision agreement — the residual is aggregation, not a bug.

A review caught Figure 10 retaining a 1/100 divisor after the export scale changed to 1/10000; the acreage mask decoded correctly throughout. One shared codec now owns the decoding and a test pins the report path to it.

Where this leaves us 22

The correction story, in one slide

  • The biggest correction is one of absence, not error — 40% of the AOI has no TreeMap value, and 204,216 ac of that carries forest evidence.
  • The cause is LANDFIRE's abandoned harvest flag — 57,798 ac of Recently Logged in 2016, zero in 2022 and 2024, with a 6–9 year lag before replanted pine is re-recognised. TreeMap is missing the whole 0–8 year managed-pine cohort.
  • 75,831 ac were returned and the result holds up twice — inside the FIA interval without overshooting (EVALIDator-verified to 0.04 ac), and corroborated by LCMS at 0.98 on sampled accepted interiors against a 0.078 floor.
  • The mechanical corrections are done — vintage disambiguated, identifiers made exact, the label-0 drop closed and tested.
  • Segmentation was the negative result that mattered — better shapes, no fewer slivers. The merge is load-bearing.
  • Two corrections are decided but unbuilt — riparian acres must stop being erased, and regimes must key to pixels while trajectories key to (plot, regime, SI).
  • The recovered acres still have no trees in them — Phase 3 has to synthesise a stand per patch before any of this reaches FVS.
The honest limit: total forest area is now validated against design-based FIA. Nothing yet validates within-forest-type attribute bias — area-scaling can correct where the acres are, never what is standing on them. TreeMap remains a mapping and landscape-modelling product, not a substitute for design-based FIA estimates.
