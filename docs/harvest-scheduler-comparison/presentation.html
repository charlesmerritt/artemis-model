Each finding is a probe, and the probe fails if the code it describes changes.

Linear against quadratic is the difference that matters. E8: below target, Ecotrust's two target forms score a (5, 5) split and a (2, 8) split identically. They price the total shortfall, not how it is spread, so under scarcity they cannot produce even flow unless the evenflow term sits beside them, as it does in the climate driver. ARTEMIS's quadratic scores the uneven split 36% worse (A5: 15.0 against 20.4). That convexity is why the 2026-09-14 plan settled into a near-constant 27–45% shortfall. The C scheduler made the same quadratic choice.

ARTEMIS · weights 06

ARTEMIS's 6 : 1 is a priority order; Diaz's 6 : 1 was an exchange rate

63.68
weighted even-flow term of the best 2026-09-14 plan

300.5

40.8%

Ecotrust scales every term to 0–100, so weights of 6 and 1 trade six points of timber for one point of carbon. ARTEMIS's standing term is a fraction of the landscape's own maximum, so its whole range is at most w_standing = 1. Even flow is a sum over 80 squared relative deviations. The scheduler will therefore never give up more than 1/6 of a unit of Σ r² for standing volume. That is less than closing one of the 80 targets from a 41% miss to zero. Standing volume acts as a tie-break.

Decision for the architect. config/projection.yaml says the weights "set relative priority" after Diaz et al. Either keep them as a priority order and say so (and stop citing the 6× as Diaz's), or normalise each term over its attainable range so that the weights trade. A carbon term built like the standing term would inherit the same position.

ARTEMIS · target keys 07

Relative deviation prices a Federal cubic foot at 35 Private ones

Each of the 80 targets is scored as ((V − T)/T)², so every key counts equally in percentage terms. Federal (NF) and Other public supply 8.4% of the five-county volume, yet they are 2 of the 8 keys, so at equal percentage misses they carry 25% of the even-flow cost. Diaz's single absolute global target had no such effect; instead it let harvest move between districts. The C scheduler ran each ownership on its own, so the question never came up there.

Equal-percent weighting is a choice. It should be a stated one. The alternative is to score absolute deviations scaled by one landscape constant. Under that scheme a Private miss dominates, and the public groups become nearly free to miss.

ARTEMIS · records against code 08

ARTEMIS's records claim four objective forms and spatial-penalty functions. The code has two forms and no penalties

Configured forms are not read A2

Objective looks weights up by metric name and reads nothing else. Setting standing_volume to form: minimize and adding a weight-100 carbon_storage objective leaves the total at 217.02. Only harvest_volume's form is checked, in load_config.

Rule for the promotion into pipeline/: raise on any (metric, form) pair the objective does not implement.

What the drivers say A2

The docstrings of 2026-08-31 and 2026-09-14 both say "All four objective forms are implemented". 2026-08-31 adds that the spatial penalties "are implemented as functions". Neither driver defines one; spatial_penalties_available returns False. config/projection.yaml describes evenflow_target as "a value or a range, and it may vary over time"; the code takes one constant per key.

Where the fix goes. weekly-artifact/ is a dated record and is not rewritten. The correction belongs to the promotion of the annealer into pipeline/, which the module docstring of pipeline/s3_management/harvest_scheduler.py says has not happened.

Spatial constraints 09

Ecotrust's history holds the adjacency check ARTEMIS says Ecotrust lacks

config/projection.yaml says "the Diaz et al. scheduler carried no adjacency constraint at all, so its code cannot supply them". That is true of the Python scheduler, where adjacency is a commented-out TODO (a harvest clump over 75 adds 1000). It is not true of the repository, whose C scheduler has a working unit-restriction check:

propose polygon p (owner own[p], prescription fixed), offset o drawn at random
if acres[p] >= 20:
    for q in adj[p] with own[q] == own[p], acres[q] >= 20, a forest stratum:
        for each period t:
            if cut[p, o, t] > 80 bd ft/ac and cut[q, offset[q], t] > 80:
                reject and draw another polygon        # a hard URM, never priced

Worth taking H2

A neighbour list (adj.txt pairs), a size floor so slivers and riparian strips do not block, same-owner scoping, and a decision unit that is a polygon linked to an FVS plot and scaled by acres. ARTEMIS's Landscape already has that last structure, so LETO polygons can carry adjacency without a new trajectory library.

Missing from it gap

Green-up spans a single 5-year period. There is no opening-size (ARM) aggregation. "Cut" is a board-foot threshold per period rather than a regeneration-harvest flag. Moves are single-polygon only, which is the stall the declared block move exists to fix.

Targets per owner 10

The C scheduler had per-ownership targets. TPO's two marginals couple ARTEMIS's

The C scheduler's harvest(ownership, target) ran one anneal per owner type (PI, STATE, FED, PNI), each against that owner's own per-period volume, with an optional boost over chosen periods. That is the per-owner-group even flow AGENTS.md asks for, decomposed. The Python rewrite went global, and Diaz et al. document the result: harvest moved between BLM districts.

Ecotrust COne target series per ownershipyes one anneal per owner

Consequence for scaling. TPO publishes only the ByCounty and ByOwnerGroup marginals. County-first or state-by-state runs, the route AGENTS.md sketches, are therefore not independent problems. Decomposing them needs either county × owner cell targets, which TPO does not publish, or a coordinating step that reconciles owner-group totals across counties.

What follows 12

Take, leave, decide

Take from Ecotrust

The C adjacency check as the starting design for adjacency_greenup, extended to a multi-period green-up window and an ARM opening-size sum.

Polygon → plot × acres as the decision unit, so LETO units reuse the library.

Independent parallel restarts reporting spread, which Ecotrust tested in 2013 and 2026-09-28 now does.

Leave already avoided

Global targets, which caused the district shift. Linear target forms with no evenflow term beside them (E8). 0–100 normalisation over zero-filled options (E5).

The warm-start and totals bugs (E1, E3) are absent from ARTEMIS (A0).

Decide architect

Are 6 : 1 a priority order or an exchange rate (#weights)? Should a Federal ft³ cost 35 Private ones (#keys)? How should runs decompose under coupled marginals (#ownership)? Are negative offsets admissible? Raise on unsupported forms when the annealer is promoted (#forms).

Closing 13

The claims, and what they rest on

  • Diaz et al. ran the Python scheduler, which has no adjacency and no per-owner targets. The repository's deleted C scheduler had both (H2).
  • The committed BLM driver uses a band, not a target, and its warm start is discarded (H1, E1).
  • Ecotrust's Python scheduler has eight reproduced defects (E1–E8). ARTEMIS avoids every one that applies to it (A0, A5).
  • ARTEMIS's 6 : 1 behaves as a priority order (A3), and a Federal ft³ is priced at 35 Private ones (A1).
  • ARTEMIS reads two of its four documented forms and silently ignores the rest (A2).
  • The limits. The C scheduler was read, not compiled. Whether E1 touched Diaz's published numbers cannot be settled from the repository. ARTEMIS's numbers come from committed 2026-09-14 outputs, with no FVS re-run. The LAMPS PDF is not in the repository, so that half of the lineage is not compared here.
