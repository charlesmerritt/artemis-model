## 1. What the PR gets right

**The citations are accurate.** I extracted the committed
`docs/references/Climate_FVS_Simulation_Report_20150306_SUBMITTED.pdf` (61 pp.) and checked
every load-bearing claim in `notes/trajectory-library-and-annealing.md`. All confirmed:

| Claim in the note | In the PDF |
|---|---|
| Four objective forms: `maximize`, `minimize`, `evenflow`, `evenflow_target` | p. 28, verbatim, including "can be defined as single value or range of values, and can be varied over time" |
| Timber target weighted 6×, everything else 1× | p. 29: "A 6x weight was set for the timber yield target, and all other weights were set at 1" |
| Offsets delay the first activity 5/10/15 yr "to offer choices to the optimization model" | p. 24, verbatim |
| Global targets caused district-shifting | p. 29 + footnote 7: "District-level timber yield targets are not enforced… BLM does not determine sustained yields… on the basis of the entire Western Oregon ownership, but rather by individual Sustained-Yield Units" |
| Figure 16 mix: 18% regen harvest, 25% no active management, 58% thinning/patch cut; 44–66% regen under high emissions | p. 45, verbatim |
| Simulated annealing, code published | p. 28: `github.com/Ecotrust/harvest-scheduler` |

That is unusually careful sourcing, and the `dimensions: ["county", "owner_group"]`
requirement — plus the test that pins it — is a direct and correct application of the
lesson in footnote 7.

**The structural-vs-priced constraint split is the right call**, and the PDF independently
confirms it: Diaz et al. enforced their absolutes (stream buffers, wilderness, Critical
Habitat) the same way, by restricting which prescriptions a land classification may draw
on. Making riparian no-entry a library of size one is strictly stronger than
`methodology-directions.md` item 2's original framing.

**Historical note:** this review originally endorsed `config/prescriptions.yaml`. After
`main` introduced an executable, tested policy pipeline, PR #21 removed that duplicate.
`config/management_regimes.yaml` is now the sole authority for prescriptions and eligible
menus; its resolver and renderer tests guard the decision space end to end.

**The superseded-docs handling is honest.** Status headers rather than deletions, no
withdrawn measurements, and `methodology-directions.md` states plainly that the run count
"changed shape" and got larger rather than burying it.

## 2. Findings on the new material

### 2.1 Every parameter grid fails to bind to `regime_templates.py` — **High**

The now-removed duplicate config's header claimed:

> Prescription families **and their parameter names** must match
> `pipeline/s4_fvs/regime_templates.py` REGIMES. […] `tests/test_config.py` **enforces both**.

Neither half is true. I fed each grid's first row straight to `build_thins`:

```
FAIL  unknown_forest/thin_from_below     KeyError: 'year'
FAIL  family_forest/thin_from_below      KeyError: 'year'
FAIL  corporate_forest/thin_from_below   KeyError: 'year'
FAIL  tribal_forest/selection_harvest    KeyError: 'start_year'
FAIL  federal_forest/selection_harvest   KeyError: 'start_year'
FAIL  state_forest/selection_harvest     KeyError: 'start_year'
FAIL  local_forest/selection_harvest     KeyError: 'start_year'
```

The config uses `year_offset` / `start_offset` / `end_offset` / `thin_offset` /
`clearcut_offset`; the builders require `year` / `start_year` / `end_year` / `thin_year` /
`clearcut_year`. That is **by design** — the note says "the generator resolves them to
calendar years per stand," and offsets-not-calendar-years is the right choice (it survives
a TreeMap vintage change). But:

- `test_prescription_families_are_implemented` checks only the `family` strings. Parameter
  names are unvalidated, so the config's claim that they're enforced is wrong.
- The missing test is the useful one: *resolve a grid row's offsets against a synthetic
  inventory year, then assert `build_thins(family, resolved)` succeeds and returns the
  expected number of `ThinDBH` operations.* That is the test that keeps the config honest
  across a rename on either side, and it is exactly the shape of the other tests this PR
  added.

Cheapest fix: correct the header sentence, and add the round-trip test. Second cheapest:
have the eligible-set expander own a documented `OFFSET_PARAMS` map so the translation is
one table rather than implicit knowledge.

### 2.2 Stated library size (6–12) does not match the config (3–10) — **Low**

Computed from the shipped grids:

| Class | Trajectories | Breakdown |
|---|---|---|
| `unknown_forest` | **3** | no_management 1, thin 2 |
| `family_forest` | 9 | 1, thin 3, selection 2, clearcut 3 |
| `corporate_forest` | 10 | 1, thin 2, plantation 4, clearcut 3 |
| `tribal_forest` | 7 | 1, selection 4, thin 2 |
| `federal_forest` | 7 | 1, selection 4, thin 2 |
| `state_forest` | 7 | 1, selection 4, thin 2 |
| `local_forest` | **4** | 1, selection 2, thin 1 |

The note §4 says "target **6–12** trajectories per stand"; the test asserts `2 <= total <=
12`. Two classes fall below the stated target. Not a defect — but `unknown_forest` at 3 is
worth a second look on its own merits: those stands get one real decision (thin at +15 or
+25) and the note says to "count and report these separately," which is harder to do
meaningfully when the menu is that thin. Either widen the range in the note or say why
these two classes are deliberately narrow.

### 2.3 The MHA screen will strip commercial thins from young plantations — **Medium**

`eligibility_screens.minimum_harvest_age`:

> A prescription whose **first removal** lands before the stand reaches this age is dropped
> from that stand's library.

In LAMPS, minimum harvest age governs **regeneration/final harvest**. Applying it to *any*
first removal means a precommercial or early commercial thin disqualifies the whole
prescription. Concretely: `corporate_forest` has `minimum_harvest_age: 20` and
`plantation_rotation` grids `thin_offset: [12, 18]`. A stand aged 0–7 at inventory — i.e.
recently regenerated industrial pine, which is a large share of north Florida — has its
entire `plantation_rotation` family dropped, *including* the clearcut leg at offset 25–35
that would have passed on its own. The screen removes the most realistic industrial
prescription from exactly the stands it best describes.

Two fixes, either fine: apply MHA only to the regeneration-harvest leg of a prescription,
or drop the offending leg rather than the whole family.

Separately, the numbers disagree with the plan they cite. The config points at
`docs/superpowers/plans/2026-07-28-lamps-scheduler-integration.md` as the source; that plan
specifies **40 industrial / 45 public**. The config has **20 corporate / 40 public** — a
factor of two on the industrial side, unexplained. The config does label these
"placeholders," which is honest, but a placeholder that contradicts its own cited source
should say so. (That plan is also still unexecutable: its Tasks 1–2 run
`git show lamps-harvest-constraints:…` and no such branch exists on `origin`. Flagged in
the prior review; unchanged.)

### 2.4 The compute estimate is optimistic by about an order of magnitude — **Medium**

The note §4: "roughly 10⁵ FVS runs. That is **hours on one workstation core and minutes
across a node**."

The repo has a measurement. `research/restart_fidelity/README.md:136-139`:

> launched 5 concurrent FVS workers; wall-clock 0.8s
> sum of per-worker time 3.0s vs wall-clock 0.8s

That is **~0.6 s per single-stand run at `num_cycle = 4`**. Library trajectories need
`num_cycle = 10`, so call it 1.0–1.5 s. Against the shipped grids (~8 trajectories for a
corporate-dominated AOI × ~10⁴ units ≈ 8 × 10⁴ runs):

| | at 0.6 s/run | at 1.2 s/run |
|---|---|---|
| 1 core | ~13 h | ~27 h |
| 16 cores | ~50 min | ~1.7 h |
| 64 cores | ~13 min | ~25 min |

So "hours on one core" holds only at the optimistic end and reads better as *most of a
day*; "minutes across a node" needs ~64 cores, not a typical workstation. Neither number
sinks the design — the note's real point, that this is a **one-time cost per library
version** rather than per scenario, is correct and is what matters. But the estimate is
quoted in the note, the pipeline plan, and `PLAN.md`, and someone will plan a week around
it.

Two things the estimate omits entirely:

- **Statewide.** Florida is 67 counties against a 5-county pilot. Even sublinearly, that is
  ~10⁶ runs — days on a node, and `PLAN.md` still targets the full eastern US after
  Florida. The note stops at the pilot.
- **The runner does not exist.** `notes/management-pipeline-plan.md:171` still reads
  "**FVS runtime**: Continue with Windows GUI handoff (proven path)." A GUI handoff is not
  a path to 10⁵ runs. The plan does add "it matters more now," which understates it: the
  whole architecture is gated on an automated parallel runner, and what exists today is a
  5-process demo in `research/`. That should be an explicit, sized work item in the
  sequence, not a stretch goal in a decisions list.

### 2.5 The regeneration gap now distorts the objective function — **High**

Prior review §3.6 flagged that `regime_templates.py` emits no regeneration keywords, so a
clearcut leaves bare ground for the rest of the horizon. Under PR #21 this stops being a
reporting problem and becomes a **search** problem.

`config/projection.yaml` now sets:

```yaml
- metric: "harvest_volume"   form: "evenflow_target"   weight: 6.0
- metric: "standing_volume"  form: "maximize"          weight: 1.0
```

`clearcut` is an eligible prescription for `family_forest` (offsets 25/35/45) and
`corporate_forest` (20/30/40), and `regime_templates.clearcut` removes 100% of TPA across
DBH 0–999. With no regeneration, a corporate clearcut at offset 20 contributes **zero
standing volume for 30 of the 50 years** to the `maximize standing_volume` term. The
annealer will therefore avoid clearcut trajectories — systematically, landscape-wide, and
for a reason that is a modeling artifact rather than silviculture. The selected
prescription mix is the PR's own headline result (§8, following Diaz's Figure 16), so the
artifact lands directly on the number the project reports.

The reference confirms this is not a corner the precedent cut. Diaz et al. (p. 27):

> a custom logic was introduced into the FVS keyfiles to limit natural regeneration to
> those species present in the stand using a similar maximum SDI threshold, with the
> density of natural regeneration based on the proportion of SDI occupied by each species
> in the stand, and the default Climate-FVS natural regeneration process was turned off.

Their regeneration harvests also retain 15 TPA (p. 22) — green-tree retention, not a 100%
removal. So the architecture ARTEMIS is following treated post-harvest re-establishment as
a first-class modeling decision and configured it deliberately.

**Fixed 2026-08-07.** `regime_templates.py` now emits an `Estab` packet with a `Plant` or
`Natural` record after every stand-replacing harvest — `clearcut` regenerates naturally by
default, `plantation_rotation` replants, and both are overridable through the regime params
(`regen`, `regen_species`, `regen_tpa`, `regen_survival_pct`, `regen_age`,
`regen_height_ft`, `regen_delay_years`). The field layout is verified against the Open-FVS
establishment keyword processor `estb/esin.f` rather than assumed, and the parser's two
silent-substitution traps (a rejected record at TPA ≤ 0, survival silently reset to 100%
outside [0.001, 100]) now raise instead.

**Species now follow Diaz et al. (2026-08-07).** Natural regeneration is limited to the
species already present in the stand and apportioned by each species' share of stand SDI
(report p. 27), via `apportion_by_sdi` from a caller-supplied `stand_sdi` mapping. Species
below a 5% share are dropped and the rest renormalised, so a trace species cannot produce a
record FVS would reject for near-zero TPA. Planting is deliberately *not* apportioned:
Diaz et al. treat it separately ("planting focused on commercial timber species following
regeneration harvests", p. 40), so `plantation_rotation` uses the configured commercial
species regardless of what stood there before. A clearcut with no `stand_sdi` still falls
back to one species, but logs a warning — that path means the Diaz rule was skipped.

**The `Natural` side effect is resolved.** esin.f FORMAT 1230: NATURAL implies
`STOCKADJ = 0.0`, `NOAUTALY` and `NOINGROW`. Planted stands now state `NoInGrow`/`NoAutAly`
explicitly, so `clearcut` and `plantation_rotation` trajectories no longer differ by a side
effect nobody chose. This is also the plain-FVS analogue of Diaz et al. turning the default
regeneration process off.

**Survival stays at 100%** by decision (2026-08-07) — it is FVS's own default rather than an
invented figure. It is knowingly optimistic: operational southern pine plantings run nearer
80–90%, so post-clearcut stocking is high across every clearcut and plantation trajectory.
Recorded in the register's `open_questions` so it cannot be forgotten at publication.

**The assumptions now have a home.** `config/fvs_keywords.yaml` is the register: the
parameter field layout of every keyword ARTEMIS emits, where each layout was verified, the
fields we deliberately leave blank, the silvicultural numbers, and an `open_questions` list
naming every value that is still a placeholder. `regime_templates.py` reads it rather than
carrying its own copies, and `tests/test_config.py` ties the two together — a keyword whose
rendered field count stops matching the register fails, as does a keyword with no recorded
verification source.

**Still not verified against a real FVS run** — no FVS binary or R runtime exists in the web
container. One smoke run of a `clearcut` keyfile, checking that TPA recovers after the
harvest cycle, is the gate before library generation, and it should confirm at the same
time that `NoInGrow`/`NoAutAly` are not no-ops under the SN variant's partial establishment
model. That is the standard `ThinDBH` was held to.

### 2.6 `applies_when: "SMZ_Pct >= 50.0"` is a string that invites `eval()` — **Low**

```yaml
overrides:
  riparian:
    applies_when: "SMZ_Pct >= 50.0"   # RIPARIAN_SMZ_PCT in regime_assignment.py
```

Nothing parses this today. It is documentation shaped like an expression, and the obvious
way to "implement" it later is `eval`/`pandas.query` on a config string. Make it structured
(`field: SMZ_Pct`, `op: ">="`, `value: 50.0`) or demote it to a comment and keep the
threshold in `regime_assignment.RIPARIAN_SMZ_PCT` as the single source, which the comment
already implies it is.

### 2.7 Small gaps in the new tests

- No guard against an **inverted `selection_harvest` grid**. `start_offset > end_offset`
  makes `range(start, end+1, interval)` empty, producing a "selection_harvest" prescription
  with zero cuts — silently a duplicate of `no_management`, and it would count toward the
  library-size budget. No current grid inverts; nothing stops one.
- `test_library_size_stays_within_the_run_budget` covers `ownership_libraries` but not
  `overrides`. Low risk given the riparian test, but the budget assertion has a hole.
- Nothing asserts that `eligibility_screens.minimum_harvest_age.by_ownership` covers every
  class in `ownership_libraries`. It currently does; a new class would screen against a
  missing key.

### 2.8 Two claims worth softening

- **Dedup hit rate is asserted, not measured.** §4: "identical `(tree list, site attrs,
  prescription)` triples are cached." Composite tree lists carry continuous weights
  (`CELL_COUNT/TOTAL_CELLS`, 5% floor, renormalized), so multi-donor units will essentially
  never hash equal. The cache will hit on **single-donor units** — where the filter leaves
  one plot at weight 1.0 — and it degenerates to exactly the `(plot, prescription)` keying
  the previous design used. Given TreeMap's median plot footprint of ~107 ac against units
  of 5–100 ac, that fraction could be large, which would make the cache *very* effective.
  Either way it is measurable right now from existing `assign_plt_cn` output, and the
  run-count estimate in §2.4 should be quoted after that measurement rather than before.
- **Unit count depends on an unresolved policy.** The "2,442 clean units" for Union County
  is the `merge` sliver policy; `sliver_merge.py`'s shipped default is `drop` (prior review
  §2.3). The library size, the run count, and the total forest area in the projection all
  move with that switch, and it is still open.

## 3. One architectural comment

The PR resolves `methodology-directions.md` items 1 and 4 as **stand = management unit**,
weighted-union tree list, one FVS run per `(unit, prescription)`. My prior review argued the
opposite — keying trajectories on `(plot, regime, SI bin)` to bound run count. **The PR's
choice is the better one and its reasoning is correct**: the weighted union has Option B's
biophysics with Option A's run structure, the partial-harvest distribution rule genuinely
dissolves, and the adviser's speckling objection is answered by construction because no
decision is ever keyed to a plot. It also matches the precedent — Diaz et al. keyed
prescriptions to 40-acre management units, not to GNN donor plots.

The cost is stated honestly (10⁵ rather than 10³ runs) and bought back three ways, two of
which hold unconditionally (barrier-free parallelism, one-time-per-version). The third —
the dedup cache — is the one that needs measuring (§2.8).

The composite-arena caveat in §4 deserves more prominence than a bullet. Pooling donor
plots into one FVS stand means trees from spatially disjoint plots compete for light and
growing space as though co-located, and FVS's density-dependent mortality and diameter
growth act on the pooled SDI. For a 20-acre unit drawing on two adjacent plots that is
benign; for a 100-acre unit drawing on five plots with different stocking it is a real
assumption doing real work. The note says "state it in the methods writeup" — it should also
be a **validation check**: compare unit-level year-0 BA/TPA/QMD from the composite run
against the area-weighted mean of the donor plots' own runs. If they diverge materially, the
composite is doing more than bookkeeping and that number belongs in the paper.
