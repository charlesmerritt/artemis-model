# Ownership bundling: pixel-share vs. dominant-owner threshold

**Status:** measured, 2026-07-26. Supersedes decision 3 of
`docs/superpowers/specs/2026-07-17-orchestrator-sketch.md`.
**Evidence:** `weekly-artifact/2026-07-26/fig7_bundle_threshold.png`,
regenerated by `weekly-artifact/2026-07-26/make_figures.py`.

## The question

The orchestrator decomposes work as **one bundle per ownership class** — federal,
state, tribal, local, and the private classes from the Harris 2025 raster. Even
flow is a constraint *within* an owner and independent *across* owners, so the
bundle boundary is also the parallelism boundary. That makes "which bundle does
this stand belong to?" the input to the whole optimization: a mis-assigned stand
corrupts an owner's flow.

The sketch answered it with **dominant-owner-with-threshold**: assign a stand to
its plurality ownership class if that class holds more than ~70% of the stand's
pixels; otherwise exclude and log.

## What the data says

Tabulating Harris 2025 ownership over the actual footprint of each of the 693
FVS stands in the five-county pilot (1,204,028 painted acres):

| Threshold | Stands assigned | AOI acres retained |
|---|---|---|
| 50% | 451 / 693 | 69.8% |
| 60% | 329 / 693 | 47.5% |
| **70%** (as specced) | **212 / 693** | **23.3%** |
| 80% | 124 / 693 | 6.5% |

At the specced threshold the rule throws away **more than three quarters of the
AOI's acreage**. Lowering the threshold does not rescue it: even a bare plurality
rule (50%) drops 30% of the acres, and by then "dominant" means very little.

### Why — this is structural, not a tuning problem

A TreeMap "stand" is **not a spatially coherent unit**. TreeMap 2022 imputes one
FIA plot ID to every forested pixel CONUS-wide (see
[How TreeMap works](treemap-methodology.md)); a `stand_cn` is therefore a *set of
scattered pixels* spread across the AOI wherever the imputation matched, not a
contiguous polygon. Ownership is spatially organised; imputed plot identity is
not. A stand's footprint straddles owners **by construction**, and no threshold
value fixes that.

### Denominator trap

Two denominators are available and they disagree materially:

- **Share of the stand's whole painted footprint** — what the spec's wording means.
  Gives the table above.
- **Share of only the owner-classified pixels** — excludes Harris classes 0–2
  (unknown-forest, non-forest, water), which cover **15% of AOI acres** inside
  painted stands. Gives a much rosier 364 / 693 stands at 70%.

The second answers a different and easier question ("of the pixels whose owner we
know, how many agree?"). Use the full footprint unless the rule is explicitly
redefined; fig 7's left panel plots both distributions so the gap is visible.

## The alternative: pixel-share allocation

Instead of assigning each stand to one owner, let each stand **contribute its
acres to every owner proportionally to its pixel counts**:

```
acres(stand, owner) = pixels(stand, owner) × 900 m² ÷ 4046.8564224
```

Across the AOI that gives, by TPO owner group:

| Owner group | Acres |
|---|---|
| Private (family + corporate + tribal) | 829,892 |
| Federal (NF) | 150,515 |
| Other public (state + local) | 41,123 |
| No owner class (Harris 0/1/2 inside painted stands) | 182,498 |

**Properties.**

- **Nothing is discarded.** All 1,204,028 acres stay in the problem; there is no
  reviewable-exclusions list to maintain because there are no exclusions.
- **It is the honest reading of the data.** A stand really is 60% family and 40%
  corporate; the threshold rule was rounding that to 100% family or to nothing.
- **The even-flow constraint still works.** The flow constraint is on *volume*,
  and volume is additive: an owner's harvest in period *t* is the sum over stands
  of that stand's per-acre volume × that owner's acres in it. Nothing about the
  LP requires a stand to belong to exactly one owner.

**Costs, stated plainly.**

- **A stand appears in more than one bundle.** Bundles are no longer a partition
  of stands, so the "one worker owns a disjoint set of stands" story breaks:
  either one worker projects a stand and its results are shared across owners'
  solves, or the same stand is projected redundantly per owner. Project once and
  share — the projection does not depend on which owner is accounting for it.
- **A cut is not divisible the way acres are.** If the allocation tells the
  federal bundle to cut 30% of a stand that is only 40% federal, the physical cut
  lands on pixels that are 60% someone else's. Pixel-share is sound for
  *accounting* the supply side; it is **not** yet sound for *applying* harvest.
  That gap is the open question below.
- **Ownership error propagates silently.** With a threshold, a bad ownership
  pixel gets a stand excluded and logged. With pixel-share it just shifts a few
  acres between owners with no signal. Worth a QA check on total acres per owner
  against an independent source.

## Recommendation

1. **Use pixel-share allocation for the supply side now** — standing volume and
   growth per owner, and the even-flow target each owner's bundle is solving
   against. This is what `fig3_supply_vs_demand.png` already does.
2. **Do not use either rule for applying cuts.** Harvest application needs a
   spatially coherent unit whose pixels share an owner. That unit is a
   **management unit**, not an imputed plot — `pipeline/s3_management` is building
   exactly that (parcel + road + stream/waterbody BMP segmentation). Bundle at the
   management-unit level once those units exist and the problem disappears: a
   management unit derived from parcels is single-owner by construction.
3. **Keep the threshold rule as a diagnostic, not a gate.** The purity
   distribution is a useful QA signal on ownership-vs-stand agreement; it is a bad
   admission criterion.

## What this changes in the spec

`2026-07-17-orchestrator-sketch.md` decision 3 ("dominant-owner with threshold …
the threshold value is itself a parameter to justify and tune") is falsified as
written for TreeMap-imputed stands: no threshold value is defensible, because the
unit being thresholded is the wrong unit. The stand-selection guard 1
("ownership assignment") should read as pixel-share for accounting plus a
management-unit dependency for harvest application.

## Open questions

1. How does a rolling-horizon LP apply a cut allocated in fractional owner-acres
   of a stand? Options: round each stand to its dominant owner **at apply time
   only** (accepting the error where the volume accounting is already correct);
   defer all application to management units; or constrain the allocation to
   select only stands above some purity so the applied cut is nearly clean.
2. Does the 15% of AOI acres Harris calls non-forest/water/unknown inside painted
   TreeMap stands indicate a TreeMap-vs-Harris disagreement worth resolving, or
   just edge effects between an ~11 m EPSG:4269 grid and a 30 m EPSG:5070 one?
   The warp is nearest-neighbour, so some of it is resampling.
3. Does the `maxstands 500` FVS limit interact with this? Under pixel-share an
   owner's stand list is every stand containing any of its pixels — potentially
   all 693 for private — which forces sub-bundling sooner than the spec assumed.
