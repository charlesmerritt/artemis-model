WHAT THIS SAYS

Flexibility closes most of the gap; Suwannee is a supply question

Open menus fix most of the miss.

Letting every owner thin, clearcut and run either rotation takes the five-county shortfall from −33% to −8% and the even-flow cost from 10.5 to 1.28.

Repeat rotations matter as much as the menu.

A stand cut once in 50 years cannot sustain TPO; replanting and cutting again inside the horizon is the other half.

Forest type costs almost nothing.

Dropping the forest-type rule entirely only improves the cost from 1.28 to 1.23. Keeping it is right.

The residual is supply.

Suwannee can reach at most 76% of its TPO total and Other public 96%, whatever the schedule.

Four things to take away. First, the owner-specific menus were the main reason the last schedule ran a third short: with every owner allowed every regime, the same landscape gets to within 8% of TPO on average. Second, repeating rotations inside the 50 years is as important as opening the menus; a keyfile that cuts a stand once cannot sustain a pine landscape's harvest. Third, keeping forest type fixed costs almost nothing, so the open menu does not need to put pine plantations on hardwood sites to work. Fourth, what remains is Suwannee and other public land, and both are limited by timber, not by the schedule. Caveats: these are pixel-class stands on the published TreeMap, not LETO units on the improved rasters; the minimum harvestable percentage is not yet enforced; and the annealer is choosing what matches TPO, not modelling what owners would choose.
