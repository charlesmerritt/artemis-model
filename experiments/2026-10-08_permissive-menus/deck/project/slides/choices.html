Share of each owner class's acres in the chosen plan · short: pine 25 yr, natural 30 yr · long: pine 35 yr, hardwood clearcut

What the annealer actually did with the freedom. Each bar is one owner class's acres in the chosen plan, split by regime family. Short rotations are the 25-year pine plantation and the 30-year natural-regeneration clearcut; long rotations are the 35-year pine plantation and the hardwood clearcut. Thinning is the light thin and thin-and-restore; selection the two selection regimes. Every class uses every family. Family forest, the largest class, is mostly on rotations, because it has to supply most of the private target. Federal land leans on thinning and selection: its target is small, so it can meet it with light entries. Grow only stays on every menu and the annealer uses it, about 45,000 acres including the 11,000 riparian acres that can take nothing else, where cutting would overshoot a target. Read this with care: the annealer picks whatever regime matches TPO, not what a family landowner would plausibly do. This is the answer to 'what would it take', not a forecast of owner behaviour.
