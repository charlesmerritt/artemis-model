The published landscape held 59% of FIA's Suwannee forest and 81% of its standing volume. Repaired ownership alone restores the volume (254M ft³ against FIA's 247M). The catch: TPO's Suwannee target is 1.8× the growth FIA measures there today.

Same menu, FVS, annealer and seeds as the deck · best of 22 searches · FIA EVALIDator 2022

Which rasters did this deck's schedule use? None of the repaired ones. Every plan on the earlier slides runs on pixel classes of the published TreeMap 2022 and the published Harris ownership raster. This slide reruns the same plan on each repaired raster set: the same menu, FVS runs, annealer, TPO targets, 22 searches and seeds. The first row is the deck itself, rebuilt: identical stands, library and greedy start, best plan 7.33 against the deck's 7.36, inside the seed spread. The second row keeps the published TreeMap but uses the repaired ownership raster. That alone adds 153 thousand acres of existing forest that Harris had left unowned or non-forest, 38 thousand of them in Suwannee, and halves the five-county miss from 8% to 4%. The four repaired sets also add back young forest, started at age 5 from the scaled establishment lists: 185 thousand acres in v1, 158 in v2, 118 in v3, 78 in v4. Every one meets the five-county TPO total to within 2.2%, and Suwannee goes from 70% of its 50-year target to between 91% (v4) and 99% (v1). The young stands cut nothing before the 2030s. What they do is let the annealer cut mature Suwannee stands harder in the early periods, because something else will carry the middle of the horizon. Why Suwannee? Its TPO average for 2013 to 2024, 22.5 million cubic feet a year, is the largest of the five, and it doubled from 2011 to 2013. FIA's own 2022 estimates put Suwannee's forest at 240 thousand acres, its growing stock at 247 million cubic feet and its net growth at 12.3 million a year. The TPO target is 1.8 times that growth, against 0.8 to 1.1 times in the other four counties, and even FIA's removals estimate is above growth. The published landscape held only 59% of that forest and 81% of that stock, which is most of why the deck ran Suwannee short. With the repaired rasters the plan meets TPO, but it cuts about 1.1 billion cubic feet in Suwannee over 50 years, against 0.86 billion of stock plus growth at FIA's current rate. So it relies on FVS regrowing managed stands faster than FIA measures the forest growing today. Caveats: stands are pixel classes, not LETO units; add-back stands start at age 5, not at their dated age; the open-menu config is a reconstruction, because the original was never committed.
